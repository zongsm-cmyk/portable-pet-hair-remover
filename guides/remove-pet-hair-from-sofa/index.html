How to remove pet hair from a sofa without damaging upholstery.

A practical routine for fabric couches and pet-friendly homes. Always check the upholstery care label first.

Start with the fabric, not the tool.

Pet hair can collect along seams, cushions and throws. Work gently: some upholstery fibers snag or pill under rubbing. If the manufacturer specifies professional-only cleaning, follow that instruction rather than trying an untested hack.

1. Check the care code and test a hidden spot

Follow the furniture makers cleaning instructions. Keep water off fabrics marked for solvent-only or professional cleaning. Before any repeated strokes or moisture, test the method on a concealed area.

2. Lift loose hair in short passes

Start with a clean, dry microfiber cloth or a vacuum upholstery attachment. Work from the back toward the front, collecting the fur as you go. Use light pressure, especially near piping, delicate weave and cushions.

3. Try an upholstery-compatible lint tool

A reusable lint brush or rubber tool may help on durable woven fabric. Test carefully first: do not use abrasive edges, hard scraping or force on velvet, loosely woven material, leather or suede. Stop if you see pulling, pilling or color change.

4. Finish the seams and maintain a simple routine

Vacuum crevices, clean the collection tool and wash removable covers only as directed by their labels. Frequent gentle upkeep is preferable to aggressive scraping after fur builds up.

Quick questions

Can I use a wet rubber glove? Only if the furniture maker permits damp cleaning and your hidden-spot test is successful; otherwise choose a dry method.

Will one tool work on every couch? No. Fabric, weave and surface treatments differ. Avoid claims of universal compatibility.
